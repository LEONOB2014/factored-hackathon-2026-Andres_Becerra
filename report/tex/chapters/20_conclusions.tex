\chapter{Discussion, limitations and conclusions}\label{ch:conclusions}

The report set out to examine every method behind the Grain Atlas and to judge, with the strictest evidence the data
allow, which machine-learning products the bank's data can support. This chapter gathers what the evidence supports,
what the data lack, what follows for the pipelines, and the limits of the report itself.

\section{What the evidence supports}\label{sec:cc-supports}

\paragraph{The data are sound in form and thin in content.} Every primary key is unique and never null, every record
of the raw files is accounted for, and under the reviewed contract the circuit breaker holds
$\est{ch07.held-global-country-all}{value}$ partitions of the main copy (\cref{ch:inventory-keys,ch:schema-forensics}).
The defects lie in the content: links documented as foreign keys that resolve to nothing, a null rendered into the text
of $\est{ch07.nan-subjects}{value}$ campaign messages, channels born and retired without open tracking, and a field of
the complaints whose values are a uniform random draw. Of the $\est{ch18.cp-total}{value}$ complaints,
$\est{ch18.cp-with-product}{value}$ name an affected product, and every one of them,
$\est{ch18.cp-other-owner}{value}$, names a product of another customer (\cref{sec:le-random}).

\paragraph{The backup is a replicate, not a copy.} It is incomplete (\ident{H4}), it does not re-key the main copy's
entities (\ident{H2}), no shift in time aligns it (\ident{H1}), its shared records were changed without a trace
(\ident{H5}): $\est{ch05.rescored}{value}\,\%$ of shared customers were re-scored without moving their audit timestamp
and $\est{ch05.redenominated}{value}\,\%$ of paired products changed currency at flat rates. Its core tables are
indistinguishable in distribution from the main copy's (\ident{H3}), so it served throughout as an independent draw of
the same process (\cref{ch:record-linkage,ch:statistical-equivalence}).

\paragraph{The process behind the data has fixed parameters.} The transaction population of every month is
indistinguishable from sampling noise around a fixed distribution (\cref{sec:sf-countries}), no level shift larger
than about $\est{ch09.mde-l2}{value}\,\%$ occurred (\cref{ch:change-points}), and after false-discovery control the
only calendar structure is the weekly rhythm (\cref{sec:time-calendar}). Contacts and complaints are Poisson at the
customer-month grain, and the learnable customer signal is the arithmetic of exposure: the dormant share falls with the
number of products held along a line on the log scale with $R^2 = \est{ch14.exp-r2}{value}$ (\cref{sec:ag2-exposure}).
Such data cannot test a model's robustness to drift, and they reward no feature that a generator did not put there.

\paragraph{One target is learnable, and a model with a pair of parameters learns it.} Of the
$\est{ch18.cells}{value}$ evaluations of the learnability map, $\est{ch18.no-signal}{value}$ show no evidence of signal.
Fraud is predictable only through a score shipped with the data that encodes the label; from behaviour alone the
out-of-time AUC is $\est{ch08.leak-auc-features}{estimate}$, interval $\est{ch08.leak-auc-features}{ci}$
(\cref{sec:ad-fraud}). Dormancy is learnable, and the gamma-Poisson model of \cref{prop:le-gp} ranks it with an AUC of
$\est{ch18.gp-auc}{estimate}$ ($\est{ch18.gp-auc}{ci}$), as well as boosting, and calibrated (\cref{ch:learnability}).

\paragraph{No model of the hour grain is ready.} The registered re-estimation gives, on the main copy,
$\est{ch19.main-green}{value}$ green, $\est{ch19.main-amber}{value}$ amber, $\est{ch19.main-red}{value}$ red and
$\est{ch19.main-not-assessable}{value}$ not assessable gates, and on the backup $\est{ch19.backup-green}{value}$ green,
$\est{ch19.backup-amber}{value}$ amber, $\est{ch19.backup-red}{value}$ red and $\est{ch19.backup-not-assessable}{value}$
not assessable. It confirms the published scorecard wherever it can and overturns $\est{ch19.disagree}{value}$ red into
not assessable, in the direction of caution (\cref{ch:readiness-gates}).

\section{What the data lack}\label{sec:cc-lack}

A red gate is a statement about missing data, and the chapters name what is missing. Each item below is a data
requirement with an owner, and each would be judged by re-running the same frozen gate on the new feed.

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[Time with a declared clock] Every process drew its days in a delivery clock that no field records
  (\cref{ch:time-and-clocks}); timestamps must carry their zone, and the day boundary of each process must be
  declared, not inferred.
  \item[Keys that link what they claim to link] Complaints cannot be traced to the contact that started them, customers
  and agents cannot be joined to a branch, and of $\est{ch11.disputes}{value}$ complaints only
  $\est{ch11.disputes-linked}{value}$ reach a link to the disputed transaction with any confidence, because the source
  never recorded it (\cref{ch:inventory-keys,ch:marts-and-features}).
  \item[Confirmed labels] The fraud label is explained by a leaked score; a label confirmed by investigation, with its
  date, is the only basis for a fraud model (\cref{ch:anomaly-detection}).
  \item[Workforce, telephony and schedules] Agent and hour gates need shifts, staffing, queue and handle-time logs that
  the extract does not hold (\cref{ch:operations,ch:readiness-gates}).
  \item[Counterparties] Without the counterparty of a payment the graph has no money-movement edges, and no graph
  feature of fraud or money laundering can be built (\cref{ch:graph-and-privacy}).
  \item[Experiments] Every causal question about campaigns needs a randomised holdout, which the marketing process
  never ran (\cref{ch:products-and-campaigns}).
\end{description}

\section{What follows for the pipelines}\label{sec:cc-pipelines}

The repairs the chapters derive are changes to code and contracts, not to models.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Contracts.} Monitor an empty share within the rows where its field applies and judge it with a binomial
  rule against a reference-window baseline; set scale baselines per currency; add an expected-partition check driven
  by the landing manifest; require that every change of a value moves its audit timestamp
  (\cref{ch:schema-forensics,ch:record-linkage}).
  \item \textbf{Monitors.} Report the smallest change a detector would have caught; give hourly limits the day-level
  dispersion; test change points on dependent series by permuting blocks (\cref{ch:change-points}).
  \item \textbf{Conformance.} Check the order of task groups statically on every pull request; convert dollar amounts at
  the source's own convention; start the first version of every slowly changing dimension at the beginning of time
  (\cref{ch:conformance}).
  \item \textbf{Marts and exports.} Capture the disputed transaction when a complaint is taken; build no feature or
  match on the complaint's affected product, which is a random draw (\cref{sec:le-random}); replace
  the salted hash of identifiers by a keyed function; set the contribution bounds of private releases without looking
  at the data and publish their noise scales (\cref{ch:marts-and-features,ch:graph-and-privacy}).
  \item \textbf{Audit.} Judge each delivery rather than the cumulative history: the current gate on approved payments
  without a response code breaches only after $\est{ch13.r03-days}{value}$ entirely corrupted days
  (\cref{ch:audit-and-gates}).
  \item \textbf{Gates.} Never raise on a degenerate input, report it as not assessable; make every benchmark
  deterministic; resample the independent unit; judge every learnable target against the simplest model of the same
  thing (\cref{ch:learnability,ch:readiness-gates}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations of this report}\label{sec:cc-limits}

\paragraph{The data are fixed.} The dataset ends on \proto{dataset.end_date}, and every notebook saw all of it. The
registered re-estimation rules out adjusting a gate's specification after seeing its clustered result; it does not rule
out choices of features, windows and benchmarks made earlier on the same data, and it adds no new evidence. A confirmatory
test of any gate needs data that did not exist when the gate was designed.

\paragraph{Most claims are exploratory.} Of the $\est{ch01.ledger-claims}{value}$ claims, $\est{ch01.ledger-registered}{value}$
are registered and $\est{ch01.ledger-exploratory}{value}$ exploratory, and $\est{ch01.ledger-naive}{value}$ of the
exploratory intervals treat dependent rows as independent and are marked \naivemark{}. Exploratory claims describe the
data; they do not decide a gate.

\paragraph{The scope of the re-estimation.} It ran once per copy, on the whole bank; the scopes per market were not
re-estimated, and on the backup the scenarios that could not run leave their gates without a verdict
(\cref{sec:rg-limits}).

\paragraph{The environments.} The registered gates ran in the \ident{eda} environment and every other claim in the
report's, and on the build platform the environments resolve some shared packages to different versions
(\cref{app:software}).

\paragraph{The data are generated.} The evidence of Parts~II and~III is that the copies are draws of a generator with
fixed parameters. The methods, their checks and the repairs transfer to a bank's real data; the specific verdicts
describe this generator and should not be read as statements about any real bank.

\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:cc-conclusions}

The bank's data are structurally sound and nearly empty of learnable behaviour. That is not a failure of the analysis:
the positive controls show that the methods find a signal where one was planted, and the registered gates show that the
verdicts survive the strictest treatment of their uncertainty. The value of the work lies in the audit. Every red gate
names why its target cannot be learned, and the cause is a property of the data that the data-engineering cycle can
change: a clock, a key, a label, a log, a counterparty, an experiment. Collecting what is missing, re-running the same
frozen gates on the new feed and building only what passes is the loop that turns this data platform into a source of
machine-learning products, of which the copilot is the first.
