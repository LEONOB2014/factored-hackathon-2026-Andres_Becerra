\chapter{Products, campaigns and the limits of causal claims}\label{ch:products-and-campaigns}

Products and campaigns are where a bank expects analytics to pay: which products will go unused, which campaigns convert,
where the next send should go. They are also where analyses most easily overreach, because every comparison of campaigns is
a comparison of the customers and channels they happened to reach. This chapter examines the product grain, the spend mix,
campaign economics, the decision cell that the granularity series proposed for allocating sends, and the time sequences
between events, and it separates what each finding shows from what it would take to make it causal.

\section{Products}\label{sec:pc-products}

At the product-month grain nothing is learnable. Whether a product used this month will go unused for the following $\est{ch17.prod-horizon}{value}$ months is
predicted with an AUC of $\est{ch17.prod-dormant-auc}{value}$, and whether a credit product is more than a month past due,
from its payment history, with $\est{ch17.prod-dpd-auc}{value}$ ($\est{ch17.prod-dpd-auc-lo}{value}$ to
$\est{ch17.prod-dpd-auc-hi}{value}$). Roll rates, the monthly transition of credit products between delinquency buckets,
cannot be built at all: days past due are a single snapshot per product, not a monthly history. The customer-level dormancy
signal of \cref{sec:cd-dormancy} disappears at the product grain because it was exposure, the number of products, not a
property of any one of them.

\section{The spend mix}\label{sec:pc-mix}

The concentration of spend across the $\est{ch17.categories}{value}$ merchant categories is measured by the
Herfindahl--Hirschman index, $\mathrm{HHI} = \sum_i p_i^2$ for category shares $p_i$ in per cent, which is $H / k$ for $k$ equal shares and $H = \est{ch17.hhi-max}{value}$ when one category takes everything. Its mean across market-months is
$\est{ch17.hhi-mean}{value}$, against $\est{ch17.hhi-uniform}{value}$ for an even split: spend is spread almost evenly. The mix
does not move: the stability index between the opening and the closing $\est{ch17.psi-months}{value}$ months is at most $\est{ch17.cat-psi-max}{value}$
(\cref{eq:sf-psi}), and month and category are independent in every market ($p \ge \est{ch17.cat-p-min}{value}$). There is no
seasonal or trend shift in what customers buy for a merchant model to learn.

\section{Campaigns}\label{sec:pc-campaigns}

Of the $\est{ch17.campaigns}{value}$ campaigns, $\est{ch17.campaigns-untracked}{value}$ ran on channels without open tracking
(\cref{sec:sf-findings}) and record $\est{ch17.untracked-conversions}{value}$ conversions: their effect is not measured, not
absent. Among the tracked campaigns, conversion rates differ far more than binomial noise allows: the Pearson dispersion of
conversions across campaigns (\cref{eq:sf-dispersion}) is $\est{ch17.phi-all}{value}$. Within each of the
$\est{ch17.channels-tracked}{value}$ tracked channels, however, it lies between $\est{ch17.phi-within-min}{value}$ and
$\est{ch17.phi-within-max}{value}$, the range of pure chance. Campaigns differ only through their channel, whose conversion
rates run from $\est{ch17.rate-min}{value}\,\%$ to $\est{ch17.rate-max}{value}\,\%$. The series found that a campaign's
conversion can be ranked before launch from its attributes (out-of-fold rank correlation $\est{ch17.launch-rho}{value}$); the
attribute that does the work is the channel. Prior sends do not lower conversion (logit slope $\est{ch17.fatigue-slope}{value}$,
$p = \est{ch17.fatigue-p}{value}$): there is no measurable contact fatigue.

\section{The decision cell and shrinkage}\label{sec:pc-cells}

The series proposed allocating sends by decision cell: channel, promoted product, objective, segment and market. A cell's
conversion rate is estimated from few sends, so the raw rate is noisy. Empirical Bayes treats the cells' true rates as draws
from a common beta distribution, whose mixture with the binomial is the beta-binomial of \textcite{skellam1948}, fits its
parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$ by maximum likelihood, and replaces each cell's rate by its posterior mean,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pc-eb}
  \tilde\theta_c = \frac{s_c + \alpha}{n_c + \alpha + \beta},
\end{equation}
which shrinks a cell with few sends towards the common mean and leaves a cell with many sends near its own rate
\autocite{efron1975}. The prior's concentration $\kappa = \alpha + \beta$ is the number of sends it is worth. Fitted to the
$\est{ch17.cells}{value}$ tracked cells, $\kappa = \est{ch17.kappa}{value}$ sends, against a median cell of
$\est{ch17.median-sends}{value}$: the data say that cells hardly differ, so the prior outweighs most cells' own evidence.

Each predictor is scored on the cells' held-out months by log loss per send, a strictly proper scoring rule
\autocite{gneiting2007}, as skill against the global rate, with intervals from resampling cells (\cref{tab:ch17-cells}). The
raw cell rate does worse than the global rate (skill $\est{ch17.skill-raw}{estimate}$, $\est{ch17.skill-raw}{ci}$): it fits
noise. The channel rate does better ($\est{ch17.skill-channel}{estimate}$, $\est{ch17.skill-channel}{ci}$), and so does empirical
Bayes centred on the channel ($\est{ch17.skill-ebc}{estimate}$), but they cannot be told apart: the difference in log loss is
$\est{ch17.ebc-minus-channel}{estimate}$ ($\est{ch17.ebc-minus-channel}{ci}$). Everything a cell adds beyond its channel is
noise, which matches the dispersion of \cref{sec:pc-campaigns}.

\section{Allocating sends}\label{sec:pc-allocation}

The series replayed allocation policies on the held-out months: each policy splits the same number of sends across cells
by rates estimated on earlier months, and is credited with each cell's realised held-out conversion rate, value and cost
(\cref{tab:ch17-allocation}). The actual mix produced $\est{ch17.alloc-actual-conv}{value}$ conversions; a random allocation
produces $\est{ch17.alloc-placebo}{value}$ on average. The bank's targeting is indistinguishable from chance. Sending greedily to
the best channel would raise conversions by $\est{ch17.alloc-channel-conv}{value}\,\%$ and cost by
$\est{ch17.alloc-channel-cost}{value}\,\%$, lowering the return on cost from $\est{ch17.alloc-actual-roi}{value}$ to
$\est{ch17.alloc-channel-roi}{value}$; the empirical-Bayes cells do the same. A proportional tilt raises conversions by
$\est{ch17.alloc-tilt-conv}{value}\,\%$ at $\est{ch17.alloc-tilt-cost}{value}\,\%$ more cost. Maximising conversions and
maximising return are different objectives, because the channel that converts best also costs most per send.

\paragraph{What the replay cannot show.} A replay credits a policy with rates observed under the actual allocation. It assumes
that a cell converts at the same rate when it receives many more sends, and that the customers who would be sent to under the
new policy behave like those who were, neither of which the data can check. Off-policy evaluation can correct for the way the
logged policy chose its sends when that choice is known \autocite{dudik2011}, but here it is not, and no observational method
recovers the effect of a send on a customer who was never left unsent at random (\cref{sec:mf-campaigns}). The decision that
would settle the question is an experiment: hold out a random share of each cell, and measure the difference.

\section{Sequences, sessions and sends}\label{sec:pc-sequences}

Does one event lead to another within hours: a digital error to a call, a decline to a call, a send to a session? For each
pair the series compares the number of consequents in a window after the antecedent with the number in the same window before
it. Across $\est{ch17.seq-tests}{value}$ pairs and windows, the ratio of after to before ranges from
$\est{ch17.seq-ratio-min}{value}$ to $\est{ch17.seq-ratio-max}{value}$, with $\est{ch17.seq-raw-sig}{value}$ $p$-values below the
conventional level even before any correction. Inside a digital session, events follow one another in random order
(Cram\'er's $V$ of $\est{ch17.funnel-v}{value}$ between consecutive events), and an error does not change the odds of a purchase
once the session's length is held fixed (odds ratio $\est{ch17.error-or}{value}$, $p = \est{ch17.error-p}{value}$): longer
sessions contain both more errors and more purchases, a confounder a naive comparison would mistake for an effect. The delay
from send to open is uniform over its window (Kolmogorov--Smirnov $p = \est{ch17.delay-ks-p}{value}$) and opening within a day
does not depend on the hour of the send ($p = \est{ch17.sendhour-p}{value}$). Send-time optimisation has nothing to optimise.

\section{Verdict and implications}\label{sec:pc-verdict}

Products carry no learnable signal, the spend mix is even and stable, and campaigns differ only by channel. A decision cell
finer than the channel adds noise, which empirical Bayes correctly shrinks away, and the current targeting performs like a
random allocation. The one robust lever, the channel, trades conversions against cost. Every causal question here (does this
campaign cause conversions, would a different allocation earn more, does an error cause a call) is unanswerable from these
data and answerable by a randomised holdout, which is the single most valuable change the marketing process could make.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:pc-limits}
