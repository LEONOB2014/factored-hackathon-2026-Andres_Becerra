\chapter{Anomaly detection}\label{ch:anomaly-detection}

An anomaly is not one thing. A record can break a rule that should never be broken; a value can be extreme for its
context; a day can carry an unusual volume; a customer can be unusual across many dimensions at once. Each needs its
own tool, and each tool rests on assumptions that decide what it can and cannot see. This chapter follows the
exploratory notebooks from rules to robust statistics to machine-learning detectors, and asks of every method the
same questions: what does it assume, how would we know if it worked, and what did it find in this dataset?

The answer to the second question shapes the chapter. The real data carry only one label, the fraud flag, and
\cref{sec:ad-fraud} shows that the behaviour the detectors see carries no information about it. Detectors are
therefore compared on a benchmark with planted anomalies, a positive control in the sense of \cref{sec:em-ledger}:
its numbers show what each method can find, never what the bank contains.

\section{Integrity rules}\label{sec:ad-rules}

The simplest detector is a predicate that should never be true: an approved payment without a response code, a
product opened before its owner existed. The notebook encodes $\est{ch08.rules}{value}$ such rules and counts, for
each, the share of the rows it applies to that violate it, in both copies (\cref{tab:ch08-rules}). A rate relative to
the rows a rule applies to is comparable across copies even when one copy is truncated.

Almost every violation is structural. Apart from one rule, the rates of the copies differ by at most
$\est{ch08.rule-gap-max}{value}$ percentage points, so the defects come from the generator that produced both, not
from the path that produced the backup. The exception confirms the reading: transactions dated before their product
opened make up $\est{ch08.rate-r01}{value}\,\%$ of the main copy and $\est{ch08.rate-r01-backup}{value}\,\%$ of the
backup, because the backup's transactions stop early (\cref{ch:inventory-keys}) and early transactions precede product
openings more often: a change of denominator, not a new defect.

The violations fall into a few kinds.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Timelines are not causally ordered.} $\est{ch08.rate-r02}{value}\,\%$ of products open before their
  customer registered, and $\est{ch08.rate-r01}{value}\,\%$ of transactions predate their product. Tenure and
  time-since-opening features are unreliable in this dataset.
  \item \emph{Mandatory fields are missing at a fixed rate.} Approved transactions without a response code
  ($\est{ch08.rate-r03}{value}\,\%$), non-dollar transactions without a dollar amount ($\est{ch08.rate-r18}{value}\,\%$)
  and branch transactions without a branch ($\est{ch08.rate-r10}{value}\,\%$) cluster at the same level, the signature
  of nulls injected at a fixed rate rather than of a process failure.
  \item \emph{A currency label is wrong.} $\est{ch08.rate-r17}{value}\,\%$ of transactions in Mexico are labelled in
  dollars, and the peso code does not exist, while transaction and product currencies always agree
  ($\est{ch08.viol-r04}{value}$ violations): the label is consistently wrong, which no per-row check could reveal.
  \item \emph{Audit stamps and attributes are implausible.} $\est{ch08.rate-r06}{value}\,\%$ of customers carry an
  update stamp after the end of the data, $\est{ch08.rate-r05}{value}\,\%$ were minors at registration, and credit
  attributes sit on products that cannot have them ($\est{ch08.rate-r08}{value}\,\%$ and
  $\est{ch08.rate-r09}{value}\,\%$).
\end{itemize}
The clock rules are a cross-check of \cref{ch:time-and-clocks}. The convention that a timestamp moved to the delivery
clock of transactions reproduces the processing date fails for $\est{ch08.viol-r14}{value}$ transactions, but for
$\est{ch08.rate-r15}{value}\,\%$ of contacts and $\est{ch08.rate-r16}{value}\,\%$ of complaints: exactly the tables
whose delivery window \cref{ch:time-and-clocks} found to start later, so the rule is right to fire and its threshold is
wrong for those tables. Location is absent on $\est{ch08.rate-r19}{value}\,\%$ of ATM and point-of-sale transactions and
$\est{ch08.rate-r20}{value}\,\%$ of digital events are anonymous; these are coverage facts that bound what any
geographic or identity feature can do.

\section{Point outliers in amounts}\label{sec:ad-points}

For a value $x$ in a group with median $\tilde x$ and median absolute deviation $\mathrm{MAD}$, the modified $z$-score
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ad-modz}
  M = \frac{\Phi^{-1}(3/4)\,(x - \tilde x)}{\mathrm{MAD}}
\end{equation}
is a robust standardisation: under normality it has unit spread, and a handful of extreme values cannot inflate its
yardstick. The conventional screen flags $\lvert M \rvert > \est{ch08.modz-cut}{value}$ \autocite{iglewicz1993}; Tukey's fences flag
values beyond the quartiles by more than $\est{ch08.tukey-k}{value}$ interquartile ranges \autocite{tukey1977}. The notebook applies both to
log amounts within each combination of transaction type and channel, since a value normal for a transfer is extreme
for a purchase.

Both screens rely on a tail. Here there is none: the dollar value of transactions never exceeds
$\est{ch08.usd-max}{value}$, and $\est{ch08.usd-at-or-above-cap}{value}$ transactions reach the conventional
ten-thousand-dollar reporting level. Amounts are a bounded mixture of bands, so the screens flag the edges of the bands,
not risk, and a structuring analysis (splitting payments to stay below a reporting threshold) has nothing to find. The
behavioural rules of card-fraud engines fare similarly: the notebook counts $\est{ch08.impossible-travel}{value}$
consecutive transactions in different countries within a short interval and $\est{ch08.rapid-repeat}{value}$ rapid
repeats, a number chance alone produces at this volume. These quantities reappear below as detector features.

\section{Abnormal days}\label{sec:ad-days}

For the daily row count $y_t$ of each table, the notebook decomposes $\log(1 + y_t) = T_t + S_t + R_t$ into trend,
weekly seasonal and remainder components by STL, the robust loess-based decomposition \autocite{cleveland1990}, and
measures the strength of the weekly pattern as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ad-strength}
  F_S = \max\Bigl(0,\ 1 - \frac{\operatorname{Var}(R_t)}{\operatorname{Var}(S_t + R_t)}\Bigr)
\end{equation}
\autocite{wang2006}. Abnormal days are sought in $R_t$ by the generalized extreme studentized deviate test
\autocite{rosner1983}: remove the most extreme value repeatedly, computing at step $i$ the statistic
$R_i = \max \lvert x - \bar x \rvert / s$ over the remaining values and the critical value
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ad-esd}
  \lambda_i = \frac{(n - i)\, t_{p,\,n-i-1}}{\sqrt{(n - i - 1 + t^2_{p,\,n-i-1})(n - i + 1)}}, \qquad
  p = 1 - \frac{\alpha}{2 (n - i + 1)},
\end{equation}
and declare as outliers the most extreme $k$ values, where $k$ is the largest $i$ with $R_i > \lambda_i$. Unlike a
fixed threshold, the procedure controls the chance of declaring any outlier in a sample of approximately normal
values.

Recomputed here on both copies, the procedure flags the same number of days as the notebook in
$\est{ch08.esd-agree}{value}$ of $\est{ch08.esd-series}{value}$ series (\cref{tab:ch08-volume}). Daily volumes are
dominated by the week: the seasonal strength lies between $\est{ch08.strength-min}{value}$ and
$\est{ch08.strength-max}{value}$ for every fact table except digital events ($\est{ch08.strength-events}{value}$),
and no series has more than $\est{ch08.esd-max}{value}$ abnormal days. The decisive test is replication. If an abnormal
day were an event in the bank, both copies would show it. They do not: the share of flagged days the copies have in
common is $\est{ch08.jaccard-max-other}{value}$ for every table but complaints, whose rows are identical in both copies
and whose flagged day coincides exactly (Jaccard $\est{ch08.jaccard-complaints}{value}$). Abnormal days are draws of the
generator's noise, not dated events, which is what \cref{ch:statistical-equivalence} predicts of independent
replicates. The test itself carries caveats: it assumes approximately normal, independent remainders, and STL
remainders of daily series are rarely free of autocorrelation, so the declared outliers are best read as a ranking. Level
shifts are the subject of \cref{ch:change-points}.

\section{Unusual customers: robust distance}\label{sec:ad-mcd}

For a feature vector $x$ with centre $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma$, the squared Mahalanobis distance
$d^2(x) = (x - \mu)^\top \Sigma^{-1} (x - \mu)$ is $\chi^2_p$-distributed when $x$ is $p$-variate normal. Outliers
inflate the classical estimates of $\mu$ and $\Sigma$ and so mask themselves; the minimum covariance determinant
estimator avoids this by computing the mean and covariance of the subset of observations whose covariance has the
smallest determinant \autocite{rousseeuw1984,rousseeuw1999}. The notebook scores
$\est{ch08.mcd-customers}{value}$ customers on $\est{ch08.mcd-df}{value}$ features and flags those beyond the
$\est{ch08.mcd-level}{value}\,\%$ quantile of $\chi^2_{\est{ch08.mcd-df}{value}}$, a cutoff of
$\est{ch08.mcd-cutoff}{value}$. It flags $\est{ch08.mcd-flagged}{value}$ customers, $\est{ch08.mcd-flagged-pct}{value}\,\%$
instead of the nominal $\est{ch08.mcd-nominal}{value}\,\%$.

The excess has more than one cause. The features are counts and ratios with heavy right tails, far from normal, so the
$\chi^2$ reference is wrong for them. And even under normality, distances computed from a robust estimate do not follow
$\chi^2_p$: the MCD estimate is computed from a subset and is less efficient, and its distances are better approximated
by a scaled $F$ distribution whose tail is heavier \autocite{hardin2005}. A cutoff taken from the $\chi^2$ table
therefore overflags even on ideal data. The distance remains useful as a ranking, and a cutoff for production should be
calibrated on the empirical distribution of distances, not read from a table.

\section{Detectors compared on planted anomalies}\label{sec:ad-benchmark}

\subsection{Design}

Without labels, detectors can only be compared on data with a known answer. The notebook follows the standard design
for unsupervised outlier detection \autocite{campos2016}: take real main-copy transactions, plant labelled anomalies
of $\est{ch08.bench-types}{value}$ types in a small share of rows, fit every detector blind on the contaminated data, and judge how well its
scores recover the planted rows. Some types are points or bursts that any detector should catch (a value above anything
in the data; a burst of foreign or rapid transactions); a low-and-slow type shifts several features mildly at once; a
dormant-reactivation type follows a long silence with a large movement. The evaluation rows number
$\est{ch08.bench-rows}{value}$, of which $\est{ch08.bench-injected}{value}$ are planted (prevalence
$\est{ch08.prevalence}{value}$), drawn from $\est{ch08.bench-customers}{value}$ customers.

The detectors span the families of the field: the robust Mahalanobis distance of \cref{sec:ad-mcd}; a Gaussian
mixture's negative log-likelihood; per-feature tail probabilities, from histograms \autocite{goldstein2012} or from
empirical copulas \autocite{li2020,li2023}; distance to the $k$-th neighbour \autocite{ramaswamy2000} and the local
outlier factor \autocite{breunig2000}; the boundary of a one-class support vector machine \autocite{scholkopf2001};
isolation forests, which score a point by how few random splits isolate it \autocite{liu2008}; and reconstruction
error, from principal components, an autoencoder and a variational autoencoder \autocite{kingma2014}.

\subsection{Metrics and their uncertainty}

With planted anomalies at a prevalence of $\est{ch08.prevalence}{value}$, the area under the ROC curve is a poor guide: it
weighs every negative equally, and almost all negatives are easy. Average precision, the area under the
precision-recall curve,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ad-ap}
  \mathrm{AP} = \sum_{k} (R_k - R_{k-1})\, P_k ,
\end{equation}
with $P_k$ and $R_k$ the precision and recall among the $k$ highest scores, concentrates on the top of the ranking,
where alerts are reviewed; a random ranking scores the prevalence \autocite{davis2006,saito2015}. The notebooks report
both metrics as point values. This report adds their uncertainty: evaluation rows of one customer share that customer's
behaviour, so intervals come from resampling customers (\cref{def:cluster-bootstrap}),
$\est{ch08.bench-boot}{value}$ times, with every detector scored on the same resamples so that differences are paired.

\subsection{Results}

The best detector by average precision, COPOD, reaches $\est{ch08.ap-copod}{estimate}$
(interval $\est{ch08.ap-copod}{ci}$), ahead of the robust Mahalanobis distance by $\est{ch08.ap-diff-copod-mahalanobis}{estimate}$
($\est{ch08.ap-diff-copod-mahalanobis}{ci}$) and of the isolation forest by $\est{ch08.ap-diff-copod-iforest}{estimate}$
($\est{ch08.ap-diff-copod-iforest}{ci}$); the robust distance and the isolation forest cannot be told apart
($\est{ch08.ap-diff-mahalanobis-iforest}{estimate}$, $\est{ch08.ap-diff-mahalanobis-iforest}{ci}$)

$\est{ch08.auc-copod}{estimate}$, while their average precisions range from $\est{ch08.ap-pca}{estimate}$ to

the top $\est{ch08.budget}{value}$ rows, the robust distance recovers $\est{ch08.recall-mahalanobis-amount-spike}{pct}\,\%$
of the value spikes but $\est{ch08.recall-mahalanobis-low-and-slow}{pct}\,\%$ of the low-and-slow anomalies
($\est{ch08.recall-mahalanobis-low-and-slow}{pct_ci}$), while COPOD recovers
$\est{ch08.recall-copod-low-and-slow}{pct}\,\%$ of the low-and-slow ones and only
$\est{ch08.recall-copod-amount-spike}{pct}\,\%$ of the spikes. The mechanisms are visible in the preprocessing. Tail
probabilities and ranks clip a value beyond the training range to the largest value seen, so a spike above everything
looks merely large; a distance on robustly scaled data keeps its magnitude. Conversely, mild shifts in several
features that include rare binary states are improbable jointly in the marginal view and unremarkable in a distance
dominated by continuous features. Dormant reactivations are almost invisible to everyone: COPOD finds

factor, poor at everything else, finds $\est{ch08.recall-lof-dormant-reactivation}{pct}\,\%$. The cause is an encoding:
the first transaction of every customer carries a placeholder gap, so a long silence lands next to a dense cluster of
placeholders and looks normal. That is a feature-engineering defect, and no detector can repair it.

\subsection{Ensembles}

If detectors see different anomalies, combining them should help. The notebook converts each score to its rank and
averages the ranks of the chosen members, or fuses them by reciprocal ranks \autocite{cormack2009}, choosing members on
a validation part of the evaluation rows and judging on a separate test part. One combination, the best single
detectors, was in fact chosen by their scores on the test part; chosen on the validation part, the same members are
selected, so the reported result stands. Recomputed with intervals on the test part, the ensemble of COPOD, the robust distance
and the isolation forest reaches $\est{ch08.ens-test}{estimate}$ ($\est{ch08.ens-test}{ci}$) against
$\est{ch08.best-test}{estimate}$ ($\est{ch08.best-test}{ci}$) for the best single member, a gain of
$\est{ch08.ens-gain}{estimate}$ ($\est{ch08.ens-gain}{ci}$). Averaging all $\est{ch08.methods}{value}$ detectors, by
contrast, collapses to $\est{ch08.all-test}{estimate}$: a rank average gives a blind member the same vote as a good
one, and the noise of the blind members drowns the signal. A small committee of different families is the right
design, and it cannot recover a type that all its members miss.

The method-level conclusions transfer to the backup: re-run there, most detectors move by at most
$\est{ch08.stab-max-other}{value}$ in average precision; COPOD moves by $\est{ch08.stab-copod}{value}$, the most
sensitive to the particular draw.

\subsection{Choosing a review budget by cost}

A bank does not review a ranking; it reviews the top $k$ alerts. Let a review cost $c_r$ and a missed anomaly
$c_m$, and let $\mathrm{TP}(k)$ be the planted anomalies among the top $k$ of $P$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ad-cost}
The total cost $C(k) = c_r k + c_m (P - \mathrm{TP}(k))$ changes by $c_r - c_m h_k$ when the $k$-th alert is
added, where $h_k \in \{0, 1\}$ says whether it is an anomaly. Smoothing $h_k$ by the local precision $\pi(k)$, the cost
falls while $\pi(k) > c_r / c_m$ and the optimal budget is where the precision of the marginal alert drops to
$c_r / c_m$.
\end{proposition}

The optimum therefore depends on the ratio $r = c_m / c_r$, and so does the best detector (\cref{tab:ch08-cost}). At
$r = \est{ch08.ratio-10}{value}$ the cheapest policy reviews $\est{ch08.cost-k-10}{value}$ alerts and saves
$\est{ch08.cost-saving-10}{value}\,\%$ of the cost of reviewing nothing; at $r = \est{ch08.ratio-100}{value}$ it
reviews $\est{ch08.cost-k-100}{value}$ alerts and saves $\est{ch08.cost-saving-100}{value}\,\%$; at
$r = \est{ch08.ratio-1000}{value}$ it reviews $\est{ch08.cost-k-1000}{value}$. Across the ratios examined,
$\est{ch08.cost-best-detectors}{value}$ different detectors are cheapest: a detector chosen by average precision is the
best one only for the cost ratio its top ranks happen to suit. The notebook charges reviews only for false alerts,
which lowers every total by the number of true alerts reviewed but leaves the optimal budget unchanged; here every
review is charged, as it is in an operation.

\section{The real label}\label{sec:ad-fraud}

The dataset ships a fraud flag on $\est{ch08.fraud-total}{value}$ transactions ($\est{ch08.fraud-rate}{value}\,\%$) and a
fraud score with every transaction. \Cref{tab:ch08-fraud-bands} shows what the score is. Every one of the
$\est{ch08.band-35-and-above-fraud}{value}$ transactions scored at the top band is fraud (Wilson lower bound
$\est{ch08.band-35-and-above}{pct_lo}\,\%$), and they are $\est{ch08.band-35-and-above-share}{value}\,\%$ of all fraud; the
band just below holds $\est{ch08.band-30-to-35}{pct}\,\%$ fraud ($\est{ch08.band-30-to-35}{pct_ci}$). Below it the fraud
rate is $\est{ch08.band-under-30}{pct}\,\%$, and where the score is missing it is $\est{ch08.band-missing}{pct}\,\%$; these
low and missing bands hold $\est{ch08.band-under-30-share}{value}\,\%$ and $\est{ch08.band-missing-share}{value}\,\%$ of all
fraud, cases no threshold on the score can reach. The score is not a feature that predicts the label; it is largely the
label, leaked into the data \autocite{kaufman2012}.

The decisive test is a model without it. The notebook trained gradient-boosted trees \autocite{friedman2001} on a
sample that keeps every fraud row; the sample holds $\est{ch08.leak-fraud}{value}$ of them among
$\est{ch08.leak-rows}{value}$ rows. Refitted here on the notebook's own split, with DeLong intervals
(\cref{eq:auc-delong}), the model on behavioural features alone reaches an AUC of $\est{ch08.leak-auc-features}{estimate}$
($\est{ch08.leak-auc-features}{ci}$, $\est{ch08.leak-test-fraud}{value}$ fraud cases in the test part), an interval that
contains the chance level, and an average precision of $\est{ch08.leak-ap-features}{value}$ against a
prevalence of $\est{ch08.leak-prevalence}{value}$. With the score it reaches $\est{ch08.leak-auc-with-score}{estimate}$
($\est{ch08.leak-auc-with-score}{ci}$), and the score alone $\est{ch08.leak-auc-score}{estimate}$
($\est{ch08.leak-auc-score}{ci}$). Amount, time, channel, geography and velocity carry no detectable information about the
label. This dataset cannot evaluate a behavioural fraud model, and any evaluation against the flag must remove the score
first and expect the low-score fraud to be unfindable.

\paragraph{The AUC interval.} For $m$ positives with scores $X_i$ and $n$ negatives with scores $Y_j$, the AUC
estimate is the Mann--Whitney statistic $\hat A = (mn)^{-1} \sum_{i,j} \psi(X_i, Y_j)$, where $\psi$ is one, a half-point
or zero as $X_i$ is above, tied with or below $Y_j$. With the structural components
$V^{+}(X_i) = n^{-1} \sum_j \psi(X_i, Y_j)$ and $V^{-}(Y_j) = m^{-1} \sum_i \psi(X_i, Y_j)$, its variance is estimated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:auc-delong}
  \widehat{\operatorname{Var}}(\hat A) = \frac{s^2_{+}}{m} + \frac{s^2_{-}}{n},
\end{equation}
where $s^2_{+}$ and $s^2_{-}$ are the sample variances of the components \autocite{delong1988}, computed in
$O((m + n)\log(m + n))$ from midranks \autocite{sun2014}.

\section{What the detectors flag in the real data}\label{sec:ad-consensus}

Fitted on the clean main sample, the notebook's complementary detectors (COPOD, the robust distance and the
isolation forest) agree on $\est{ch08.consensus-alerts}{value}$
rows ($\est{ch08.consensus-pct}{value}\,\%$) when a majority of them must flag a row at their own extreme tail. The
flagged rows are bursts of declined, pending or reversed transactions, often abroad: a recognisable pattern of card
testing, built from rare discrete states rather than from amounts. They are not integrity defects: approved payments
without a response code are rarer among them ($\est{ch08.r03-alerts}{value}\,\%$) than among the other rows
($\est{ch08.r03-rest}{value}\,\%$). Rules and detectors catch different problems, and a bank needs both. The kind of row
flagged replicates in the backup, whose profile of flagged rows correlates with the main copy's at
$\est{ch08.profile-rho}{value}$, although the rows themselves differ: structure replicates, individual records do not.

\section{Verdict and implications}\label{sec:ad-verdict}

The integrity rules find the dataset's real defects, all structural: broken timelines, fixed-rate nulls in mandatory
fields, a wrong currency label, implausible stamps. They belong in the ingestion gates. Point outliers, abnormal days and
unusual customers carry little information here, because amounts are bounded, daily noise is independent between copies
and the features are far from the distributions the textbook cutoffs assume; their cutoffs need calibration on data,
not tables. On planted anomalies the detectors are complementary, a small diverse ensemble beats every member, and the
choice of budget and detector depends on the cost of a miss. On the real label nothing behavioural works, because the
label is explained by a leaked score. For later chapters this has a direct consequence: fraud is not a learnable target
in this dataset, and the readiness gates must say so.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:ad-limits}

The planted benchmark measures recovery of the anomalies its design plants; absolute numbers depend on that design, and
only relative orderings and failure modes generalise. Its intervals resample customers but treat the planting itself as
fixed. The robust-distance flag count, the consensus profile and its correlation between copies are the notebooks'
printed results; the rule rates and the stability of detectors come from their stored tables. The daily-outlier test,
the benchmark metrics with their intervals, the costs and the fraud analysis are recomputed here. The supervised models
are refitted on a single split, so their intervals describe the test sample, not the variation from refitting.
