\chapter{Knowledge retrieval}\label{ch:retrieval}

A customer's general question (how to recover money lost to a transfer scam, which law protects their data) is
answered from the governed knowledge base of \cref{ch:system}. Retrieval must find the right document, find nothing
when no document applies, and never return a document that is not approved and in force.

\section{Questions and retrievers}

\est{kb.questions}{value} questions in Spanish and Portuguese were written against the documents, which are in
English. \est{kb.dev}{value} form a development split (\est{kb.dev-unanswerable}{value} of them unanswerable) used
only to choose each retriever's relevance threshold; the other \est{kb.test}{value} are the test split:
\est{kb.test-answerable}{value} with a right document and \est{kb.test-unanswerable}{value} that no document answers.

All the retrievers read the same governed chunks with the same multilingual embeddings
\parencite{wang2024e5}, which match a Spanish or Portuguese question to an English passage:
\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[bundled] an in-process index built from the documents, the retriever the copilot serves;
  \item[pgvector] cosine search over the governed view in Postgres;
  \item[graph] vector seeds in \ident{Neo4j} expanded through the entities (controls, rules, regulators, regulations) the
  seed chunks mention, restricted to active versions;
  \item[hybrid] the pgvector and graph rankings fused by reciprocal rank \parencite{cormack2009}.
\end{description}
Each threshold is the midpoint between the lowest top score of an answerable development question ranked correctly
and the highest top score of an unanswerable one. Below it, the copilot says it has no document.

The served retriever ranks the right document first for \est{kb.bundled-hit1}{num} of the
\est{kb.bundled-hit1}{den} answerable questions (interval \est{kb.bundled-hit1}{ci}) and among its top results (\ident{hit@3}) for
\est{kb.bundled-hit3}{num} (interval \est{kb.bundled-hit3}{ci}); its mean reciprocal rank is
\est{kb.bundled-mrr}{estimate} (interval \est{kb.bundled-mrr}{ci}). pgvector returns the same rankings, as it should
with the same embeddings and chunks. The graph retriever ranks the right document first slightly less often
(\est{kb.graph-hit1}{num} of \est{kb.graph-hit1}{den}), and the hybrid matches the bundled index. With
\est{kb.test-answerable}{value} answerable questions the intervals are wide and overlap: the test does not separate
the retrievers on ranking.

No retriever returned a document outside the active set: \est{kb.bundled-violations}{value} governance violations
for the bundled index and \est{kb.graph-violations}{value} for the graph retriever, the same for the others. Drafts,
superseded, retired and expired versions never reached a ranking.

The weak point is rejection. At its threshold the served retriever rejected \est{kb.bundled-rejected}{num} of the
\est{kb.bundled-rejected}{den} unanswerable questions: each scored above the threshold, so the copilot would go on to
the next step with an irrelevant passage. The graph retriever, with a higher threshold, rejected
\est{kb.graph-rejected}{num} of \est{kb.graph-rejected}{den} (interval \est{kb.graph-rejected}{ci}) at the cost of
keeping fewer answerable questions (\est{kb.graph-kept}{num} of \est{kb.graph-kept}{den}). The thresholds rest on
only \est{kb.dev-unanswerable}{value} unanswerable development questions, too few to place a boundary, and the
embeddings' scores for related and unrelated passages overlap.

What protects the customer here is the step after retrieval. A passage from an internal document sends the case to a
person; an answer written from a public passage must cite it and pass the grounding check, or the case goes to a
person; without a model, the copilot only points to the document. An irrelevant passage can therefore produce a
needless hand-off, an unhelpful pointer, or a cited answer that does not address the question; the grounding check
guarantees the citation and the numbers, not the relevance. A better threshold needs more unanswerable questions in
the development split.

Latency is small next to a model call: the served index takes a median of \est{kb.bundled-ms-p50}{value}\,ms, with
a slow tail (\est{kb.bundled-ms-p95}{value}\,ms at the \proto{stats.high_percentile}th percentile) from a few
queries scattered through the run, whose cause the run does not record.
