# ADR-010 · Federated graph learning across countries

Status: accepted (2026-10) · revised 2026-10-05 · related [ADR-004](ADR-004.md), [ADR-007](ADR-007.md),
[ADR-009](ADR-009.md), [ADR-012](ADR-012.md), [ADR-018](ADR-018.md)

## Context
Some risks are only visible across countries: mule networks, merchants abused in several markets, customers who move
money between them. A model that sees one country's graph misses them. Residency (ADR-007) forbids moving
row-level customer data across borders, so a centralised graph is not an option in production.

## Decision drivers
- **Rows never leave their residency silo;** only model parameters travel.
- **The cost of federation is measured, not assumed:** every run is compared with local-only and centralised
  training.
- **Privacy of the updates themselves** (DP-FedAvg, ADR-009).
- **The same code** for simulation now and production later.

## Decision
1. **Clients:** one per country silo. Each trains a two-layer GraphSAGE on its customer–merchant bipartite graph
   (`graph.fgl_silo_nodes` and `fgl_silo_edges`). Merchants are public nodes, replicated per silo.
2. **Server:** FedAvg weighted by client size, with optional DP-FedAvg (per-client update clipping plus Gaussian
   noise).
3. **Every run trains three variants:** local-only, federated and centralised. Each is reported per silo, so the gap
   between federated and centralised is the measured price of residency.
4. **Production:** Flower with secure aggregation, with the DP-FedAvg parameters tuned with model risk. Today's loop
   is a single-process simulation used for evaluation.
5. **Promotion follows the readiness gates** (ADR-012, ADR-018): no federated model serves decisions until it beats a
   transparent benchmark out of time.

## Implementation
- `platform/libs/latam_platform/ml/federated_gnn.py`.
- `platform/airflow/dags/ml_graph_learning.py`, with results logged to MLflow.
- dbt: `platform/dbt/models/graph/fgl_silo_nodes.sql` and the silo edges (with residency metadata).

## Evidence and verification
**Measured** ([chapter 07 §7.3](../07_ml_and_graph_learning.md)):
- task: a complaint in the next 90 days, with point-in-time cutoff and 3.6 % positives;
- 5-round smoke test, AUC per silo (AR/CO/MX):

| Variant | AR | CO | MX |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local-only | 0.49 | 0.49 | 0.50 |
| Federated | 0.51 | 0.50 | 0.49 |
| Centralised | 0.48 | 0.50 | 0.51 |

**Every variant is at chance.** Complaint behaviour is not encoded in the synthetic graph, so there is no signal for
any model, federated or not. This matches the exploratory phase's finding that the generator draws these relations
independently (ADR-015, ADR-018).

## Consequences
- **Positive:**
  - a working, measured residency-preserving learning mechanism;
  - the cost of federation will be known the day real data arrives;
  - no rows cross borders.
- **Negative:**
  - federated training is slower and harder to debug than centralised training;
  - DP-FedAvg trades accuracy for privacy;
  - secure aggregation adds infrastructure.
- **Risks:**
  - presenting the mechanism as a model with value. Mitigated: results are reported at chance, and promotion is gated
    by the readiness rule.

## Alternatives considered
- **Centralised training in one region** (rejected): it violates residency.
- **Local-only models per country** (kept as a baseline): compliant and simple, but blind to cross-border patterns.
- **Sharing embeddings instead of weights** (rejected): embeddings of customer behaviour count as personal data under
  the residency policy's forbidden outputs.

## Revisit when
- **Real counterparty or cross-border data arrives** with signal: re-run the three variants, then gate on the
  readiness rule.
- **Production federation is built:** Flower with secure aggregation, with per-silo compute in each residency region.
