# ADR-007 · Residency strategy per country

Status: accepted (2026-10) · revised 2026-10-05 · realised for serving by [ADR-021](ADR-021.md) · related
[ADR-006](ADR-006.md), [ADR-009](ADR-009.md), [ADR-010](ADR-010.md), [ADR-016](ADR-016.md)

## Context
The bank serves customers in Mexico, Colombia and Argentina, with Brazil in scope for regulation. Each country's data
protection law restricts where personal data is stored and processed, and how it crosses borders:
- Mexico: LFPDPPP 2025 and CNBV;
- Brazil: LGPD and CMN 4,893;
- Colombia: Ley 1581 and the SFC cloud rules;
- Argentina: Ley 25,326 and BCRA.

Mexico and Brazil have in-country Google Cloud regions; Colombia and Argentina do not.

## Decision drivers
- **Personal data stays where the law allows,** by construction rather than by procedure.
- **One code base for every country** (ADR-011), with residency as configuration.
- **Explicit legal dependencies** where no in-country region exists.
- **Analytics and learning across countries** without moving rows.

## Decision
1. **One policy file is the source of truth:** `platform/policies/residency.yaml`.
   - MX: `northamerica-south1`, in country.
   - BR: `southamerica-east1`, in country.
   - CO and AR: `southamerica-east1` under an **international-transfer basis pending legal sign-off**.
2. **The policy is enforced in four places:**
   - the Terraform org policy `gcp.resourceLocations` per environment;
   - dbt model metadata (`meta.residency: customer_country`, the default for every model);
   - the `publish_serving` DAG;
   - the regional copilot deployments (ADR-021), each holding only its countries' data.
3. **Environments:** `mx`, `br` and `latam-shared` (shared services and public reference data only).
4. **What may cross borders:** differentially private aggregates (ADR-009), model weights without memorisation risk
   (federated learning, ADR-010) and public reference data. Row-level customer data, embeddings of customer text and
   raw logs with personal data are forbidden.

## Implementation
- `platform/policies/residency.yaml`.
- `platform/infra/terraform/gcp/envs/{mx,br,latam-shared}` with `modules/org_policy`.
- `platform/dbt/dbt_project.yml` (governance defaults);
- `platform/airflow/dags/publish_serving.py`.
- `copilot/deploy/cloudrun/` and `copilot/scripts/scope_snapshot.py` (ADR-021).

## Evidence and verification
- **Terraform:** validate and tflint are clean for every environment ([platform README](../README.md)); the
  environments are not applied.
- **The copilot's regional deployments** show residency at runtime:
  - the Mexico service holds only MX data, the São Paulo service only CO and AR;
  - a customer of one region gets HTTP 401 from the other's service;
  - build source, image and service all stay in the region (ADR-021).
- **Locally, residency is simulated:** silo columns and per-country schemas on one laptop, as the policy file's
  `local_demo` note states.

## Consequences
- **Positive:**
  - compliant by construction where in-country regions exist;
  - residency is reviewable in one file and testable at runtime;
  - cross-country analytics and learning have a defined, privacy-preserving path.
- **Negative:**
  - per-country environments multiply infrastructure and cost;
  - cross-country features need federated or differentially private mechanisms instead of a join.
- **Risks:**
  - **Colombia and Argentina depend on a legal opinion** that is not yet given. If it is negative, those countries need
    a different hosting arrangement before production.

## Alternatives considered
- **One region for all countries** (rejected): violates residency for every country but the host.
- **On-premises hosting in Colombia and Argentina** (deferred): satisfies residency, at the cost of a second platform
  to operate. It is the fallback if the transfer basis is refused.
- **Pseudonymise and centralise** (rejected): pseudonymised data is still personal data under these laws.

## Revisit when
- **Legal sign-off for Colombia and Argentina arrives,** or a Google Cloud region opens in either country.
- **Brazilian customer data enters the platform** (none in the current dataset).

## Revision note
The original text listed the strategy without the enforcement points, the forbidden outputs, the pending legal
dependency as an explicit risk, or the runtime evidence from the regional deployments.
