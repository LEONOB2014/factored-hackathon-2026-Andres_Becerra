# ADR-021 · Serve the copilot from the residency region of its customers

Status: accepted (2026-10)

## Context
The residency standard (`knowledge/std-data-residency`, ADR-007) says a country's customer data is stored and
processed only in the regions approved for that country:
- Mexico: `northamerica-south1` (Querétaro);
- Brazil: `southamerica-east1` (São Paulo);
- Colombia and Argentina: no in-country region; the proposed region is São Paulo, under a documented
  international-transfer basis pending legal sign-off.

The first deployment of the copilot (Modal) runs outside these regions with every country's demo data in one place.
That is acceptable for a demo on synthetic data, but it does not show the rule the platform claims to follow.

## Decision
1. **One copilot service per residency region, each holding only its countries' data.** The demo snapshot is cut by
   country (`copilot/scripts/scope_snapshot.py`). The country-scope idea of ADR-016 is applied at serving time.
   - `beta-aid-mx` in Querétaro serves Mexico.
   - `beta-aid-sa` in São Paulo serves Colombia and Argentina (and Brazil when it has data).
2. **Everything that carries the data stays in the region:**
   - the build source is staged in a private bucket created in that region (the default Cloud Build bucket is US
     multi-region);
   - the build runs in that region;
   - the image goes to that region's Artifact Registry repository;
   - the service runs there.
3. **Residency is visible, not claimed.** `/api/health` and `/api/demo` report the region and the countries whose data
   the instance holds, and the UI shows them. A customer of another region is unknown to the service (401).
4. **One warm instance per region** (min = max = 1): conversations, sessions and the demo's operational store live in
   the process.
5. **Modal stays as the quick demo link,** documented as not residency-compliant.

## Consequences
- `copilot/deploy/cloudrun/deploy.sh <scope> <region> <countries…>` reproduces a regional deployment.
  `copilot/eval/live_check.py` verifies one end to end: 16/16 on both regions at deployment.
- Two always-warm services cost more than one. A production design would move sessions and actions to a regional
  database and scale to zero.
- A customer must reach the service of their country. Routing (by country of residence at login) belongs to the
  identity layer in front of the services, which this demo does not implement.

## Alternatives considered
- **One service with all countries' data in one region** (rejected): it breaks the residency rule for every country
  but the one hosting it.
- **One image without data, reading from a regional bucket at start-up** (deferred): a cleaner separation of code and
  data, but more moving parts for a demo whose snapshot is 1 MB.
