# ADR-009 · Scope of differential privacy

Status: accepted (2026-10) · revised 2026-10-05 · related [ADR-006](ADR-006.md), [ADR-007](ADR-007.md),
[ADR-010](ADR-010.md)

## Context
Aggregates that leave the trust boundary can re-identify individuals, especially small groups and repeated queries.
"Leaving the trust boundary" means across borders (ADR-007), to external parties, or into broadly shared analytics.
Operational decisions and statutory reports need exact numbers. Fraud, AML and credit decisions cannot be noised, and
regulators require exact reports.

## Decision drivers
- **Formal guarantees** where data leaves the trust boundary.
- **No utility loss** where exactness is required.
- **A privacy budget that cannot be overspent,** with every spend auditable.
- **A cloud path** that reuses the same discipline.

## Decision
1. **Where DP applies:**
   - aggregate releases across borders or to external parties;
   - synthetic data;
   - federated model updates (DP-FedAvg, ADR-010).
2. **Where DP never applies:**
   - fraud, AML and credit decisions;
   - statutory and regulatory reports;
   - the bank's own operational analytics inside the residency boundary.
3. **Mechanism:**
   - OpenDP's discrete Laplace measurement over **public group keys**, with inputs contribution-bounded by dbt;
   - epsilon taken from OpenDP's privacy map, never from a hand formula;
   - suppression of small noisy counts as post-processing, which costs no budget.
4. **The budget is debited before release** in an append-only ledger (`privacy.budget_ledger` in the audit store). A
   release that would exceed `privacy.budget_remaining` for its dataset is refused, and the refusal is recorded.
5. **Cloud:** BigQuery's differential-privacy clause for analytics views (`bq_dp_*` models, ADR-006), under the same
   budget policy.

## Implementation
- `platform/libs/latam_platform/privacy.py` (`debit_budget`, `dp_count_release`, `ensure_budget_policy`,
  `release_manifest`).
- `platform/airflow/dags/dp_release.py`, reading `privacy.privacy_input_complaints_country_month` built by dbt.
- `platform/dbt/models/privacy/` and `platform/dbt/models/bigquery/bq_dp_complaints_country_month.sql`.
- The audit schema: `privacy.budget_policy`, `privacy.budget_ledger`, `privacy.budget_remaining`.

## Evidence and verification
- **The `dp_release` DAG writes each release** with its manifest (mechanism, epsilon, delta, requester) to the
  privacy zone, and the debit to the ledger.
- **Over-budget requests are refused,** with the reason stored ("remaining epsilon … < …").
- **The library tests** (Platform Tests in CI) cover the privacy module alongside the audit controls.

## Consequences
- **Positive:**
  - formal guarantees exactly where data leaves the boundary;
  - exact data where decisions need it;
  - every spend of privacy budget is auditable and the budget cannot be exceeded.
- **Negative:**
  - noised aggregates lose accuracy for small groups, and suppression hides them;
  - analysts must request releases rather than query freely across borders.
- **Risks:**
  - budget exhaustion blocking legitimate releases. Mitigated by budget policies per dataset and period, reviewed with
    model risk.

## Alternatives considered
- **DP everywhere** (rejected): it breaks decisions and statutory reports.
- **No DP, with k-anonymity thresholds only** (rejected): k-anonymity fails under repeated and composed queries; it
  carries no formal guarantee.
- **Synthetic data only** (rejected as the sole mechanism): useful for development, but synthetic data needs DP itself
  to give guarantees, and it does not replace exact releases.

## Revisit when
- **The first external or cross-border consumer is onboarded:** set its budget policy with legal and model risk.
- **The cloud is applied:** confirm the BigQuery DP clause's accounting matches the ledger's.
