# ADR-014 · Timestamps carry an explicit clock

Status: accepted (2026-10)

## Context
No document states the time zone of the source timestamps. The platform assumed each country's legal time:
- `enrich_transactions` derives `transaction_ts_local`, `local_hour` and `is_weekend` from the transaction country's
  legal UTC offset;
- the country series placed Colombia on UTC−5 and Argentina on UTC−3.

The hour grain (`eda/notebooks/granularity_time/01`) measured the clock instead:
- **Two batch windows.** Every process belongs to its daily delivery batch. `timestamp + offset` gives exactly
  `process_date` for 100 % of rows (99.8 % of digital events):
  - offset −6 h for transactions, digital events and campaign sends;
  - offset −8 h for contacts and complaints.

  Surveys use a ~42 h window with no fixed offset.
- **The weekly rhythm follows the batch.** It is drawn on that delivery day, the same in every market. The hour ×
  weekday chi-square per degree of freedom is about 1 at the delivery offset, against about 20 at legal local time.
- **The legal clock created artefacts:**
  - weaker Colombian and Argentine weekends (−31 % against −39 %);
  - an Argentine "Monday dip" (−6.8 %, which is −1.6 % on the business clock).
- **R15 and R16 test the wrong window.** These integrity rules (contacts and complaints) test
  `timestamp − 6 h = process_date`. Their ~8.3 % violations are the 2 of 24 hours between the −6 h and −8 h windows,
  not bad data.

Evidence: [strategy 12 §12.1](../../strategy/12_development_path.md) E13.

## Decision
1. **The source contract declares the clock of every timestamp column.** It names the UTC offset or zone, and the
   delivery window that maps a timestamp to `process_date`. For this dataset:
   - `business` clock, UTC−6, for transactions, digital events and campaign sends;
   - UTC−8 for contacts and complaints;
   - no fixed window for surveys.
2. **Derived local-time columns use the declared clock**, not an assumed legal time:
   - `*_ts_local`, `local_hour`, `is_weekend` and the calendar join;
   - `enrich_transactions` takes the offset from the contract seed;
   - legal local time is kept only where the source says the timestamp is local.
3. **The business day of a fact is `process_date`.** Day-grain aggregates group by it. The timestamp is shifted only
   where an hour inside the day is needed.
4. **R15 and R16 test each process's declared window** (−8 h for contacts and complaints).
5. **A clock-drift control runs per process and month.** It records the best shift of the hour × weekday scan and the
   delivery-day conformance (the share of rows where `timestamp + declared offset = process_date`). A change holds the
   affected partitions (severity B).

## Consequences
- `is_weekend`, `local_hour` and every calendar feature change for Colombia and Argentina, and models and marts that
  read them are rebuilt.
- R15 and R16 drop to about 0 % violations. A residual is then a real data fault.
- **Real feeds must state their clock.** A feed that sends true local time declares it, and the same code serves it
  (`latam_eda.country.utc_offset(code, clock="local")`).
- The EDA already uses the declared clocks (`country.PROCESS_DAY_OFFSET`). The platform macro and the rules change in
  v0.4.0 (§12.7 P0 item 9).

## Alternatives considered
- **Keep legal local time** (rejected): it contradicts the measured rhythm and creates country differences that do not
  exist.
- **Use UTC everywhere** (rejected): day boundaries would fall inside the delivery window, splitting each business day
  in two.
- **Infer the clock at run time** (rejected as the primary mechanism): the scan is a control, not a contract. A clock
  must be declared so that a change is detected rather than silently absorbed.
