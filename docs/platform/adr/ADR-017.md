# ADR-017 · A multi-grain star on top of gold

Status: accepted (2026-10)

## Context
Gold delivers facts at the grain the source sends: one transaction, one contact, one send. A bank decides at other
grains: a customer this month, a market today, a branch's cash tomorrow, a complaint case, a campaign cell, a queue
this hour. Three notebook series rebuilt the star at those grains with header-contracted SQL run by
`latam_eda.granularity.Star`:
- **Series I** (`eda/notebooks/granularity`, `granularity_sql/`): 17 day and month aggregates, 43 / 43 checks.
- **Series II** (`granularity_time`, `granularity_time_sql/`): the hour calendar, the market-hour monitor and the
  campaign decision cell.
- **Series III** (`granularity_hour`, `granularity_hour_sql/`): 13 hour-grain dimensions and facts, 44 / 44 checks.

Every aggregate reads existing dbt relations only (`int_transactions_enriched`, `fct_interaction`, `fct_complaint`,
`fct_campaign_send`, `int_customer_profile`, `fct_digital_session`, staging and dimension models), plus two seeds: the
country calendar and the declared clock (ADR-014).

## Decision
1. **The aggregates become a dbt layer, `gold/aggregates`, in schema `agg`,** in three folders by grain family:
   `day` (day and month, series I), `cell` (hour calendar, market-hour and campaign cell, series II) and `hour`
   (series III). Each model keeps its SQL; `{name}` references become `ref()`.
2. **Each model's header contract becomes dbt tests:**
   - `grain` → the grain columns are unique together;
   - `reconcile` → every additive measure sums exactly to its atomic fact;
   - `dense` → the model has a row for every member of the declared cross product.
3. **Measures are additive.** Rates are stored as numerator and denominator and derived as a ratio of sums;
   snapshot attributes (balances, days past due) are never stored as monthly measures.
4. **All aggregates are built; only some are served.** Promoted to marts and serving (from the series' promotion
   tables):
   - customer-month, customer snapshot, market-day and channel-day, branch-day, the case, campaign-day and the
     campaign cell;
   - the declared clocks, the branch schedule and the case clock at the hour.

   Kept in the lab: agent-day KPIs (unreliable and employee data), product-month (needs DPD history), category-month,
   the customer daypart profile. Hourly volumes are served by streaming monitors, not tables.
5. **Every scope builds the same aggregates** (ADR-016, ADR-020); tags `grain:day`, `grain:cell` and `grain:hour`
   select them.

## Consequences
- The notebooks' checks become build failures. A lost row or a broken reconciliation stops the run instead of
  shrinking a KPI.
- KPIs have one definition, owned in the repository (the series' KPI catalogues).
- Build time grows. The aggregates run after gold, and the hour family can be scheduled less often than day.
- The notebooks keep their own copy of the SQL until they are switched to read `agg.*`; until then the dbt models are
  the source of truth for the platform and the notebooks for the analysis.

## Alternatives considered
- **Keep the aggregates in notebooks** (rejected): every team would rebuild its own numbers, with no single
  definition of a KPI.
- **A semantic layer only** (deferred): metrics over gold without materialised aggregates would hide the grain and
  density tests, which caught real defects. A semantic layer can sit on `agg.*` later.
- **One wide customer-day table** (rejected): 99 % of customer-days are empty, and the decisions live at other grains.
