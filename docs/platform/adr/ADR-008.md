# ADR-008 · Crypto-shredding to reconcile immutability and erasure

Status: accepted (2026-10) · revised 2026-10-05 · related [ADR-001](ADR-001.md), [ADR-003](ADR-003.md),
[ADR-007](ADR-007.md)

## Context
Two obligations conflict:
- **Immutability:** audit records, the hash-chained ledger and WORM anchors must never change, so tampering is
  detectable.
- **Erasure rights:** every regime in scope gives data subjects a right to erasure, with exceptions for legal holds
  such as AML records and transaction ledgers:
  - ARCO cancellation in Mexico;
  - LGPD art. 18 in Brazil;
  - Ley 1581 in Colombia;
  - Ley 25,326 in Argentina.

Deleting a row from a hash chain breaks the chain; never deleting breaks the law.

## Decision drivers
- **Chains must stay verifiable** after any erasure.
- **Erasure must be provable** and recorded.
- **Legal holds override erasure,** and the refusal is itself recorded.
- **Derived copies are covered,** not only the audit store.

## Decision
1. **Envelope encryption per subject.** Personal fields inside immutable records are encrypted with AES-GCM under a
   per-subject data key. That key is stored wrapped by a key-encryption key: from the environment locally, a KMS/HSM
   key that never leaves the HSM in production. The ledger client only inserts, verifies and anchors.
2. **Erasure destroys the subject key (crypto-shredding).** Records stay in place and the chains stay valid, but the
   personal fields become unreadable for that person.
3. **Derived operational stores are purged:**
   - the Postgres serving tables;
   - the online feature state;
   - Neo4j.
4. **The lakehouse is handled at rebuild:** the subject goes on a suppression list. The production path is row-level
   deletes on Iceberg tables from silver onward.
5. **Requests flow through `dsar.request`.** Requests under a legal hold are not erased; the block is logged. Every
   erasure is a ledger event plus an actioned `data_subject_erasure` compliance trigger.

## Implementation
- `platform/libs/latam_platform/audit_ledger.py` (`_subject_key`, `encrypt_for_subject`, the key-encryption key).
- `platform/airflow/dags/retention_and_erasure.py`.
- `platform/docker/postgres-audit/sql/02_audit_schema.sql`: `keys.subject_key`, `dsar.request`, `ledger.*`,
  `compliance.trigger_event`.

## Evidence and verification
- **On the end-to-end run, `verify_chain` reports 0 problems** with anchors in WORM storage ([platform
  README](../README.md)), and chapter 04 documents the tamper tests.
- **The library tests in `platform/libs/tests` cover the audit ledger and its controls,** run in CI (Platform Tests).
- **The erasure DAG records** what was purged per store and why a request was refused.

## Consequences
- **Positive:**
  - immutability and erasure coexist;
  - erasure is provable (the key is gone, the event is chained);
  - one mechanism for every immutable store.
- **Negative:**
  - key management becomes critical: losing the key-encryption key loses every subject's data, and leaking it
    defeats the scheme;
  - every read of a personal field pays a decryption.
- **Risks:**
  - personal data written outside the envelope, for example free text in a payload. Mitigated by the PII guard on
    free text before storage (the PII handling standard) and by the rule that tokens only exist from gold onward.

## Alternatives considered
- **Deleting audit rows** (rejected): breaks immutability and the chain.
- **Never erasing** (rejected): non-compliant in every country in scope.
- **Redacting in place with a re-chain** (rejected): rewrites history and needs trust in whoever re-chains, which is
  exactly what the chain exists to avoid.

## Revisit when
- **The lakehouse moves to Iceberg:** replace the suppression list with row-level deletes.
- **The cloud is applied:** move the key-encryption key into Cloud KMS or an HSM per residency region (ADR-007), and
  test key rotation.
